💬 What You Shared in the Session
"I think I need to find the average speed for the whole journey… maybe just add the two speeds and divide by 2? But I'm not sure if that's right when the distances are the same."
🧠 AI Analysis of Your Thinking
✅ Good Instinct
You correctly identified this as an average speed problem — that's the right starting point!
⚠️ Common Trap
Simply averaging the two speeds (60+40)/2 = 50 only works when time is equal, not distance. Since time differs here, this gives the wrong answer.
🎯 Key Insight Needed
Average speed = Total Distance ÷ Total Time. You need to calculate the actual time taken for each part of the journey first.
📐 Approaches (Select to explore)
1
Direct Formula Method
⭐ Recommended Fastest
Use the harmonic mean formula for average speed when distance is equal in both legs:
Average Speed = 2ab / (a+b), where a and b are the two speeds.
This directly gives the correct answer without computing time separately.
2
Step-by-Step Time Calculation
📚 Conceptual Best for Understanding
1. Assume a total distance (say, D km each way).
2. Calculate time for each leg: Time = Distance ÷ Speed.
3. Total time = time going + time returning.
4. Average speed = Total distance ÷ Total time.
This builds full understanding of why the formula works.
3
Algebraic Proof Approach
🔥 Advanced Deep Understanding
Let D = one-way distance. Express time for each leg symbolically, substitute into the average speed formula, simplify algebraically to derive the harmonic mean. This proves why the simple average doesn't work and when it does.
💡 Why simple average fails here

Because the train spends more time at the slower speed (40 km/h) than at the faster speed (60 km/h), the slower leg "weighs" more in the total time. Simple average (50) ignores this time imbalance. The harmonic mean accounts for it correctly.

Average Speed = 48 km/h
The average speed for the entire round trip is 48 km/h — not 50, because the train spends more time travelling at the slower speed.

📐 Step-by-Step Solution
1
Find time for outward journey
Distance = 120 km, Speed = 60 km/h
Time₁ = 120 ÷ 60 = 2 hours
2
Find time for return journey
Distance = 120 km, Speed = 40 km/h
Time₂ = 120 ÷ 40 = 3 hours
3
Calculate totals
Total Distance = 120 + 120 = 240 km
Total Time = 2 + 3 = 5 hours
4
Apply Average Speed formula
Average Speed = Total Distance ÷ Total Time
Average Speed = 240 ÷ 5 = 48 km/h ✓
⚡ Formula Shortcut (for equal distances)
Average Speed = (2 × a × b) / (a + b) = (2 × 60 × 40) / (60 + 40) = 4800 / 100 = 48 km/h ✓ This is the Harmonic Mean formula — use it whenever distances for both legs are equal.
🔍 Verification

Check: If average speed = 48 km/h for 5 hours → total distance = 48 × 5 = 240 km ✓
This matches our total (120 + 120 = 240), confirming the answer is correct.

🎓 Key Takeaways
✅ Average speed ≠ arithmetic mean of speeds (unless time is equal for both legs)
✅ When distances are equal, use harmonic mean: 2ab/(a+b)
✅ Always verify: (average speed) × (total time) must equal total distance
